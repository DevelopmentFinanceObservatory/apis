The Search engine resolves natural-language queries, structured parameters, and resubmitted specifications into grounded dataset specifications (`spec_id`). It integrates Data Commons knowledge graphs, Gemini structured extraction, SDMX availability checks, and the `materialise` runtime to produce typed dataset coordinates and inline data previews.

## Architecture

The resolution lifecycle processes queries through six sequential stages:

```
Query -> Extract -> Recall -> Shape -> Bind -> Materialise -> Ground -> Spec
```

1. **Extract**: Parses natural-language input into structured components, identifying target variable phrases, geographic entities, temporal constraints, and role axes.
2. **Recall**: Queries the indicator resolver on our Data Commons and on the UN System Data Commons in parallel, keeps the candidates the origin catalogue admits, and ranks each Data Commons' candidates by cosine similarity score.
3. **Shape**: Maps recalled variables into structural sentence shapes, defining required slots across dimensions (`what`, `how`, `where`, `when`, `source`).
4. **Bind**: Grounds identified entities, geographic areas, dates, units, and facet coordinates against knowledge graph nodes and SDMX dimensions.
5. **Materialise**: Evaluates observation availability across candidate slices and selects valid series coordinates using `materialise` graph and SDMX readers.
6. **Ground**: Assembles the canonical `Spec` definition, computes the deterministic content hash (`spec_id`), and returns the typed `ResolveResponse`.

Recall runs once per variable, and Shape through Ground run in up to two passes, one per pool of candidates (`region/pipeline.py`): our Data Commons' first, then the UN System Data Commons'. A superseded variable among ours stands as its successor: the UN variable the catalogue gives it (`Catalogue.successor`), at the rank and score our resolver gave the superseded one (`clients/graph.py`, `standing_dcid`). The same holds for a superseded leaf one of our Topics expands to (`retrieval/topics.py`). Several superseded variables with one successor make one candidate at their best score, and one with no successor is dropped. The successor is the UN variable of the same series code whose constraint pairs equal the superseded variable's, order ignored; a series the registry excludes, or a variable with no such UN variable, gives none (`origin_catalogue/successors.py`). A superseded hit or Topic leaf holds its place in the recall cut whether it is replaced, merged or dropped, so supersession adds no candidate the cut would have left out (`cut_hits`, `topic_leaves`). Derivation then confirms every hit inside the cut in one arc read (`derive_shapes`). A superseded variable is a base variable held only under a provenance the registry excludes as `superseded_by_un_system`. `dc/base/UN_SDG` is superseded by `undata/p/SDG`, so no base SDG data is served on any door, with the UN origin on or off, and the same series is served under `undata/p/SDG` when it is on. With the UN origin off nothing is replaced.

Our pass derives and elects over our candidates alone. The UN System Data Commons' resolve and the derivation of its shapes run as one task beside it (`ResolveState.un_leg`), each shape stamped with its pool (`ShapeDraft.pool`). When our pass ends `no_data` for a reason other than the request's entities (`entity_not_resolved`, `entity_ambiguous`, `entity_set_too_large`, `entity_role_not_admitted`) or `source_ambiguous`, or answers on its resolution without party words on a pick its binder rated "none of the shapes" above (`BindResult.none_first`, a doubted answer), and the part's source scope allows a UN System Data Commons provenance and did not settle the answer by its exclusions, a UN pass runs the same phases over the UN pool's shapes, less a shape of the id our pass elected that holds only that shape's members, when our pass found that shape without observations or answered on it doubted (`pipeline._fill_gap`). Its answer stands when it is `definite` or `candidates`, unless both binders doubted their picks. Of two refusals, the UN pass's stands when ours is `variable_not_resolved` and the UN pass elected a shape whose measured score is present and outside the weak-score band (`_representative_score_is_weak`); otherwise our answer stands, and the trailing reads of the answer that does not stand are cancelled. A part our pass answers undoubted never waits for the UN leg, and each pass's menu, admissibility and status rule read one pool. With `SEARCH_UN_ON_FIRST_MENU`, our pass's menu also holds the UN pool's shapes (`phase_intake._merge_un_pool`): a part whose best shape scores below `phase_intake.UN_MERGE_WEAK_SCORE` (0.7) waits for the UN leg and merges its shapes, and a part with a stronger shape never takes the UN pool on its pass, however soon the leg finishes. The UN shapes meet the same admissibility and their own pool's score floor, and take the seats ours leave, since the two resolvers score on their own scales (`seating.seat_menu`); a UN shape and ours of one measure are two readings to the status rule. A weak part whose leg failed or is not in by the wait answers from our shapes with `un_system_unavailable` whatever its status, and a merged pass that ends `no_data` still runs the UN pass, while a doubted answer on a merged pass runs it over the UN shapes that took no seat on its menu, and none when every UN shape took one. Each pool meets its own score floor, `SEARCH_UNKNOWN_SCORE_THRESHOLD` for ours and `SEARCH_UN_UNKNOWN_SCORE_THRESHOLD` for the UN pool, since the two resolvers score on their own scales, and each pass's bind menu holds its pool's first `SEARCH_BIND_TOP_K` shapes in rank order, each showing its origin. The UN pass, and under `SEARCH_UN_ON_FIRST_MENU` a weak part's merge, waits for the UN leg at most `SEARCH_UN_FIRST_PASS_WAIT_S` from the start of our derivation; on the UN pass a leg that failed or is not in by then leaves our answer standing with `un_system_unavailable`, which keeps the response out of the response cache. A part that only excludes sources keeps every table the code index does not declare: the read admits only the provenances its scope allows (`origin_catalogue.Catalogue.scoped`), so a reading whose only data is an excluded source has none where the question asks, and the status rule treats it so.

A part may name a publisher's series code in `ParsedPart.source_code`, such as the World Bank's DT.TDS.DEGG.CD. It names the StatVars whose `sourceIndicatorCode` arcs carry it, which the release's code index records under each provenance holding it (`CodeIndex.source_code_members`), and those StatVars are the part's recall in place of either resolver (`engine/source_code.py`, `interpret.recall`). Shape derivation, the by-creditor twin election and bind then run on them as on any recalled pool, so `open_roles: ["creditor"]` elects the by-creditor variable and no open role the aggregate, and the part gets no second pass. A code no variable it names can answer the part from refuses `source_code_not_held`, its message naming the cause (`source_code.NotHeldCause`): no source the index holds it under, the part's sources leaving out its holder, the catalogue or graph serving none of its StatVars, or its StatVars unable to answer the part as asked. That covers every exit that would refuse `variable_not_resolved` (`ResolveState.no_data`) and a Spec whose identity the Datasets mint would read as StatVars outside the code's (`pipeline._within_source_code`). Bind gets every constraint value the code's StatVars share as a sole option (`bound_values.bind_member_values`), so the minted identity elects them alone, and a variable text that is only the code reaches the bind model as its StatVars' graph name. A code two of the part's sources hold refuses `source_code_ambiguous` until the part names one, and one whose StatVar another code of its source names too refuses `source_code_shares_variable`, naming that code. Each entry of a Spec's `resolved.stat_vars.source_codes` carries `exact: true` when a part naming that code and its provenance would answer with the same Spec (`source_code.stands_for_code`, called from `spec_build._resolved_stat_vars`); an entry without it names a StatVar the answer serves, and the answer may hold other series or data of another source. On the raw-text door a query token that writes a held code fills the field (`source_code.scan_source_codes`), and so does a token shaped as a dotted series code that the index does not hold, such as the World Development Indicators code NY.GDP.MKTP.KD (a two-letter topic and two or more segments of two to five capitals or digits). That part refuses `source_code_not_held` naming the code, where the rest of its phrase would otherwise answer without it.

A UN candidate's node facts come from the catalogue's UN tables (`origin_catalogue.graph.OriginGraphClient`), so deriving its shape sends the UN deployment no node request, on a first answer and on a resubmit. A UN shape holds the variables recall found, as any shape does, with the slots and members they carry. The UN deployment is still read for the resolve, observations, SDMX availability and the label of a facet's unit.

Every provenance on the wire names its source and the Data Commons its data comes from (`dfo`, `base` or `un_system`), read with its label from the request's origin catalogue (a provenance the catalogue does not list keeps its graph label and has no source or Data Commons): `resolved.facets.provenance`, each series' `facet.provenance` and `source_refs`, and `trace.resolved_sources`. Each of these provenances also carries `documentation`, the Graph API route of its document, `/api/graph/documentation/{dcid}`, except a `per_series` resolved facet, which names no provenance. Each shape lists the Data Commons holding data for its members in `data_commons`, and a shape whose members all come through one upstream source names it in `upstream`. SDMX serves DFO's role-bearing variables only, so the role-series read raises `UpstreamVariableRoutedToSdmx` before it sends a variable the catalogue places upstream.

## Submodules

The search package comprises three submodules:

### `engine`

The core orchestration runtime for resolution and HTTP surface mounting.

- **`core.py`**: Coordinates the end-to-end resolution flow. `resolve_events` provides asynchronous event generation for streaming responses; `resolve_async` provides buffered JSON resolution.
- **`region/`**: Manages per-variable resolution (`region/pipeline.py`), the status rule that decides which readings a part offers (`region/status_rule.py`), and facet compatibility verification.
- **`retrieval/`**: Interfaces with the Data Commons knowledge graph to discover statistical variables, expand topics, and verify series coverage.
- **`clients/`**: Encapsulates upstream communication with Data Commons graph APIs (`graph.py`'s contract, `graph_client.py`'s implementation over the shared `dc_graph.GraphClient`), SDMX endpoints (`sdmx.py`), Google Gemini models (`llm.py`), and the `materialise` subsystem (`materialise_bridge.py`).
- **`options/`**: Implements candidate enumeration for entity and facet roles, allowing clients to query valid peer and child selections on demand.

### `models`

Pydantic models defining the search contract and wire representations:

- Status literals: `definite`, `candidates`, `no_data`, `unsupported`.
- Grammar slot axes (`what`, `how`, `where`, `when`, `source`) and facet axes (`provenance`, `measurementMethod`, `unit`).
- Graph references (`GraphRef`), served and posted as `{ref, label}` with the node's dcid in `ref`, the shape the Graph API serves. `ProvenanceRef`, an `ambiguous_entities` candidate and an entity group (`EntityGroup`) carry the same `ref` and `label`.
- Provenance refs (`ProvenanceRef`, with `SourceRef` and `DataCommonsRef`) and the shape's `ShapeUpstream`.
- Request envelopes (`ResolveRequest`, `OptionsRequest`) and response envelopes (`ResolveResponse`, `OptionsResponse`).

### `eval`

The eval case model, the checks that score a response against it, and the paired gate. Cases live in `corpus/*.yaml`; `corpus/README.md` describes the format.

- **`cases.py`**: The `Case` model, the loader that expands measure families, the eligibility rules, and `expectation_hash`.
- **`checks/`**: One module per check (`disposition`, `measure`, `entities`, `provenance`, `request`, `operations`, `refusal`, `warnings`, `assumptions`, `candidates`, `parts`, `skills`, `entry_path`, `structural`, `groundedness`, `materialisation`, `task_outcome`). `score` runs them over one raw resolve response and returns a verdict (`pass`, `fail`, `unverifiable`, `unreached` or `n/a`) per check. `unreached` marks a check the expectation asserts but that never ran, because the response's status or kind is one the case does not accept, or an expected part is missing.
- **`runner.py`**: Posts each case to a running app's `/api/search/resolve`, retries infrastructure failures and degraded answers, and picks the modal sample of several by vote.
- **`gate.py`**: The paired regression rule that turns per-sample verdicts on head and base into one verdict per case and check.
- **`summary.py`**: Renders the driver's report as Markdown or terminal text.
- **`live_clients.py`**, **`graph.py`**: The graph, SDMX and coverage readers the groundedness and materialisation checks use.
- **`dataset.py`**, **`sync.py`**: Build a case's request identity, and sync cases to Langfuse datasets.

## HTTP Endpoints

The search surface mounts under `/api/search`.

Every response, success or refusal, carries `x-dfo-trace-id`, `x-dfo-commit`, and a `Server-Timing` header carrying `total`. `diagnostics.trace_id` and `diagnostics.engine_build` in a success body match the header pair, and `diagnostics.timing_ms.total` matches `Server-Timing`'s own `total`, except on a streamed response, where headers go out before the stream and the terminal `done` event's `elapsed_ms` carries the total instead.

### `POST /api/search/resolve`

Resolves a natural language query, structured parse, or base specification resubmission into a dataset specification.

Requests with `Accept: text/event-stream` return a Server-Sent Events (SSE) stream emitting pipeline step events, candidate discoveries, and the final response envelope. Other requests receive buffered JSON responses.

`response_options.include_observations` is off by default because it triggers a per-spec data fetch. Set it to `true` after the engine has selected a definite specification. Buffered JSON embeds the preview in `resolved.observations`; SSE emits it in a trailing `observations` event after `response`. A multi-candidate response retains each candidate's metadata and coverage but does not fetch observation previews. A candidates part carries `observations_omitted` on both transports. Under a converting unit request every preview converts its points to the unit asked, including the bulk previews: one latest point per place for an answer over a defaulted place set, and, for a role-bearing answer over the pair cap, the most recent and largest series (`observations.order` is `ranked`). Above `role_bulk_rank_max_rows` rows, or under a conversion into each entity's own currency, the preview ships a fixed selection instead (`fixed`), StatVars newest year first and taken round-robin. Each converted series carries its `conversion`. A bulk preview leaves out a measure whose points do not convert and names it in an `observations_converted_scope_unsupported` warning, or omits the preview with that reason when no measure converts. A read that fails or crosses the deadline while a bulk preview converts omits the preview with `observations_bulk_fetch_failed` or `observations_timed_out`. When a preview fetch fails, the omission names the read that failed (`graph`, `sdmx` or `upstream response`). An exact `resolved.coverage` carries `evidence`: `facet_metadata` means `observation_count` comes from the graph's facet index and no series ships in that payload, and `observed` means a preview series ships beside it, including a partial delivery where some requested series failed and an `observations_probe_failed` warning names the read that failed. The count is the facet-index count either way. The SSE `response` event always reads `facet_metadata`, because it precedes the fetch.

`response_options` carries `include_observations`, `include_option_coverage` and `include_skills`. A request that posts `max_candidates` or `include_sentence` refuses `request_invalid`, 400: the candidate cap per part is the server setting `SEARCH_MAX_CANDIDATES`, and every spec carries its sentence. `include_option_coverage` gates slot coverage and entity-option labels only. `resolved.facet_options` is on every definite spec and every candidate; over SSE with the flag set it arrives in trailing `facet_options` events instead of the `response` event.

A role-bearing spec (one whose StatVars declare a donor/recipient flow, e.g. CRS financing) carries an exact `resolved.headline`: the observation, series, and per-role dcid counts in the latest year any of its served series has data, the same counts for that year and the two calendar years before it (`recent_years`, newest first, a year with no data kept at zero so that a latest year far below the ones before it reads as incomplete, and stopping at the first year the release's coverage view represents), whether the request itself named that year, and a sibling `resolved.coverage_stamp` naming the coverage version those counts were confirmed against. Both fields are computed from the release's coverage view (an in-memory lookup, not a graph or SDMX read), so they cost nothing extra regardless of how broad the answer is; a query that names no donor or recipient at all -- the widest unbound CRS ask -- still resolves `definite` with an exact headline rather than refusing `scope_too_broad`, the same way a narrower, named-role ask does. Both are `null` on a non-role-bearing spec.

#### The `parts` response shape

`ResolveResponse` is a union discriminated on `kind`. A `resolution` carries one entry in `parts` per variable the query asked about, in query order; a `catalogue` answer carries `scope`, and a `declined` answer carries `reason` and `scope`, with no parts. Each part is a complete outcome: it carries its own `status` (`definite`, `candidates`, `no_data`, or `unsupported`), under that status exactly the payload a single-variable response carries (`spec`, `candidates`, `no_data`, or `unsupported`), and the `operations` the question asks for over its data. The resolution's own `status` reads the parts' shared status when every part agrees, or `mixed` when two or more parts differ. A single-variable query returns one part, so `parts[0].status` equals the resolution's `status`.

A raw-text question that extract classifies as `catalogue`, `out_of_scope` or `requires_analysis` returns straight after extract, with no recall, bind or second cache lookup; `out_of_scope` and `requires_analysis` answer `declined` with that `reason`. `scope.place` is the one country the entity resolver confirms among the places the query names, read in one resolver call under the request deadline. No place, a city, a region, an organisation, two countries or more places than a resolution may name leave it null, and so does a resolver failure or timeout, which also keeps the answer out of the response cache. The stream carries `step(extract)`, `response` and `done`, with no `interpretation`, `shape` or `sentence` event. The first cache layer stores these answers, so a repeated "what data do you have?" is served from it. A question of any of the three kinds that names a measure resolves, so text in the query cannot decline a data question, and a resolution with no measure answers `no_data` with `variable_not_resolved`. The `parsed` and `dataset_resubmit` doors run no extract and always answer a resolution.

Two parts that resolve to the same five-tuple shape (`population` and `total population`, say) collapse onto one spec: both parts carry the same `spec_id`, and every part but the first carries a part-scoped `binding_folded` warning naming the fold. A candidates part carries `observations_omitted` when the request set `include_observations`, since a candidates part has not committed to one spec to fetch observations against; `max_candidates` applies per part.

A spec identity records the resolved selection. A part that names a table binds that table as `facets.provenance`. An exclusion ("not from CRS") is not an identity facet. When excluding a table changes which table serves, the identity binds the table that serves. When it changes nothing, the identity is the plain question's and mints the same `spec_id`, so the exclusion does not persist if the serving order later changes.

A family of series with no total, such as a WHO series published only by sex or by wealth quintile, answers along the breakdown the request names. Its identity carries the slots the request binds and those it opens, by an operation or by a phrase after "by", "across" or "for each" that names a slot ("under-five mortality by wealth quintile"), and leaves every other what or how slot out, so the answer and a mint of its definition serve the same members and the same rows. An identity slot left out therefore means no member the Spec serves asserts it. With nothing opened, a family whose members all break down one way answers definite over that breakdown, warning `family_total_absent` that it shows the family by those slots with a `closest_match` departure, and a family that breaks down several ways answers candidates, one per breakdown, each beside the first carrying basis `breakdown` (`retrieval/standard.breakdown_decision`, `region/phase_place_materialise._settle_breakdown`).

A warning that concerns the whole request (`variables_clamped`, a dropped query qualifier) sits on `diagnostics.warnings`. A warning that concerns one part (`binding_folded`, `observations_omitted`, or that part's own resolved warnings) sits on that part's own `warnings` list.

Every part also carries `restatement`: a `ParsedPart` reproducing that part's own resolution inputs (entities, resolved time window, unit text, source text, operations, open roles), as if it had been posted fresh to the `parsed` door. The reproduction is canonical rather than verbatim: a raw_text part's `time_text` is the phrase the engine's own time grammar re-parses back into the identical window, which can differ from the user's original wording. The guarantee covers the resolution inputs and `spec_id`, not the wording. It is null on a `dataset_resubmit` entry's `identity`/`candidates`/`placeholder` kinds, which post an identity or stub rather than a phrase; a `dataset_resubmit` `parsed` entry carries a real one, like any parsed-door part. A leg bound on a directional role axis (origin/destination) echoes that axis, per entity, as the `role_text` a client can post straight back: `from` for the origin axis and `to` for the destination one, chosen from the same lexicon `role_axis_for_text` reads. A leg's own open roles echo as `open_roles` words, `origin` or `destination`, and the axes its operations open ride on the operations. It is null when the leg's direction came from its own wording alone and no door-supplied role named the axis, when a single name was recorded on both axes and so cannot be reproduced by one role_text, and when one of the leg's own inputs sits outside `ParsedPart`'s field bounds. The withholding applies to re-parsing an edited phrase. For a definite part, the client still holds `spec.definition.identity` and can edit that entity's role directly through a `dataset_resubmit` `identity` entry.

Beside it, `entity_refs` lists each entity text of the restatement once, in restatement order, with the dcids the answer binds for it (`place_roles.bound_entity_refs`): "G7" bound as its total gives `GroupOfSeven`, and a set bound as its members gives its own ref first (its node, else its total, else its registry id) and then the members, so G7 read as members gives `GroupOfSeven` and each member. A part refused before its places were bound gives each text that resolved to one node that node, so Netherlands keeps `country/NLD` beside an ambiguous Congo, and a group or a set read as its members `[]` (`place_roles.single_node_dcids`). An ambiguous or unresolved text and every text of a soft-deadline placeholder give `[]`. A candidates part states the elected reading's binding, and a folded part its own. `restatement` itself stays a `ParsedPart`, so a client posts it back verbatim.

A part's `operations` are the things the question asks done with its data that search does not do, such as a ranking or an explanation. On the raw-text door extract assigns each operation to one variable, so only that variable's part carries it. Its phrase goes to that variable's bind in the same block as the dropped qualifiers, so "top 5 donors for education aid" lets bind leave the donor axis open and the part covers every donor. An operation raises no `query_qualifier_dropped` warning and adds no note to the sentence. The parsed and `dataset_resubmit` doors echo the operations each posted part carries. A request part carries at most 8, the most extract keeps for a whole query.

A part asks for every member of a flow role in one of two ways: `open_roles` on a `ParsedPart` lists at most two `role_text` words (`creditor`, `donor`, `recipient`, `debtor`, `origin`, `destination`), and an operation whose `over` is `origin` or `destination` opens that axis on every door. A role named in neither the part's entities nor its open roles is the total over its members, so a question that names no recipient or creditor reads the series published without one. An open role joins the election only where a member the bound roles alone elect, or a member at the same constraint values such as the by-creditor twin of a total, declares it: search elects those members, the answer holds one series per member, and the identity states the axis in `open_roles`. Otherwise the question is read as if the role were unnamed, and the identity omits an open role its elected members declare no role on; on a role-bearing answer the part warns `query_qualifier_dropped` that the breakdown was not applied. A word that names no role, or a role an entity of the same part binds through `role_text`, refuses `request_invalid` at `/input/parts/{i}/open_roles/{j}`. An entity the part binds on the axis without `role_text` wins over the open role: the open role drops and the part warns `query_qualifier_dropped`. On the raw-text door, a phrase that asks for one value per member of a role opens that role as `open_roles` does: "by donor", "across recipients", or an "each" or "every" phrase ("from each donor", "for each donor", "every provider"), whether extraction drops it, names it as a place, or reads it as an operation. An "each" or "every" phrase opens the role under any operation, and "by" or "across" a role only where no operation of the part but a rank claims it, whether the phrase is the operation's own or a dropped one, so "total aid to Kenya across donors" keeps the total (`role_text.breakdown_axis`, `role_text.every_member_breakdown_axis`, `raw_text._breakdown_under`). Such a phrase raises no warning of its own and runs no operation, so "ODA to Kenya by donor" departs no `operation_not_applied`. A sum or other operation tagged "by donor" or "across donors" keeps its operation and departs `operation_not_applied` as any operation Search does not run. A dropped "all" phrase ("all donors", "to all creditors", or "from all countries", whose preposition names the side) opens no axis, and a role-bearing answer that binds no place on that axis meets it: one series per member, or the total over them. An "each" or "every" phrase in an operation's own words on the parsed door asks for the breakdown, so only an answer that declares a role on that axis meets it (`role_text.every_member_breakdown`), and a candidate set meets a phrase only when every candidate does. A met phrase raises no `query_qualifier_dropped` warning and no departure. The warning is request-wide, so it stays while no part of the request meets the phrase, and each part whose own answer leaves the phrase unmet carries a `measure_not_applied` departure for it: in "aid to Kenya and GDP of Kenya from all donors" the aid part meets "from all donors", the response carries no warning, and the GDP part departs (`role_text.every_member_axis`, `region.result.every_member_phrases`, `assemble.unapplied_qualifier_warnings`).

Two tables that publish one structural question are chosen between before bind. Each table reads the query in its own codes, and it reads a phrase when its rows hold a code the phrase names, a phrase naming several codes included (`retrieval/bound_values.query_phrases_read`). Of two families of one five-tuple and role class, the one reading fewer of the query's phrases leaves, whether or not the code index carries them (`elect_five_tuple_family`). "Imputed multilateral ODA to basic education" reads the sector only on ONE's imputed sectors table, so DAC2a's imputed total leaves. A table can also state a word of every row it serves without coding it (`DatasetDeclaration.stated_words`): every CRS row is bilateral, since the import leaves out core contributions to multilateral organisations, and a DAC2a or DAC2b amount is bilateral where its recipient is a country. Such a word restricts nothing on those rows, so a part reads it as it reads a word of the table's label (`region/phase_rank_bind._dataset_stated_words`), and "bilateral ODA to Kenya" answers as "ODA to Kenya" does. The part states the word where it modifies the measure ("net bilateral ODA"): a negated or excluded occurrence ("non-bilateral", "excluding bilateral flows") and one past a function word, a connective or a separator ("ODA net of bilateral flows") are not read (`bind_repair.word_stated`). In the election a family reads a word it states beside a rival the code index carries that reads the word in no phrase, so "bilateral ODA from Germany by recipient" leaves DAC2a, whose open recipient axis holds multilateral organisations, for the CRS. Beside a rival that codes the word a stated word adds nothing, so "bilateral ODA from France" stays DAC1's bilateral aid type, and it adds nothing beside a family the index does not carry, so a debt series by bilateral creditors compares with the CRS as it does without the word. The graph's text search matches series names, which hold no stated word, so recall searches a text holding one a second time without it and adds the series of the tables that state it (`interpret._detect`). A table can also declare that a word names one of its parties (`DatasetDeclaration.party_words`): "multilateral" names `DACMultilaterals`, the DAC's published total of the multilateral organisations, as the provider of a DAC2a or DAC2b amount to a country. A part states such a word as it states a stated word, on a flow axis the request leaves unnamed: no place is directed to it, no operation runs across it and the part does not ask every member of it, while a name is directed to each axis the declaration reads a kind on (`region/place_roles.stated_party_words`). Such a part is read with the word first (`region/pipeline.resolve_variable`). Recall also searches the text without the word for the series of the tables that declare it, intake reads the party's node as a place set (`region/phase_intake._party_word_sets`), and a shape whose every dataset declares the word binds the party on that axis beside the places the query names, where the shape reads each word next to the word (`held_party_words`, `_beside_words_read`, bound in `bind_place_roles`). The party's set passes role admission as a named set does. The reading stands where a shape binding the party reads every phrase any other pooled shape reads, the word counted as read; the binding shapes are then the pool (`region/phase_rank_bind.with_party_reading`). "Multilateral ODA to Kenya" answers DAC2a's flows from the multilaterals total, as "ODA from multilaterals to Kenya" does. The reading is withdrawn where no shape binds the party ("multilateral development bank ODA to Kenya"), where another shape reads a phrase the binding shapes do not (the CRS sector of "multilateral aid for education to Kenya"), where another shape reads the word as a value of its own, in a phrase of its codes or by a graph label ("multilateral loans to Kenya", a World Bank IDS creditor type), and where the answer it reaches does not hold the party in every Spec. Each spec's own `resolved.rendered_sentence` is a heading and a byline. The heading states what the answer is, in possessive order with one slot token per axis: "Germany's gross ODA disbursements to Kenya, 1973 to 2024". A unit that changes the magnitude and is no currency (per capita, percent of GDP, PPP international dollars) stays in the heading. The byline carries the latest-year label when one applies, the currency with its price basis ("current US dollars", "constant 2024 euros", "current Guatemalan quetzales", `unit_words.currency_nouns`) as a token with axis `unit`, and the source. The measure noun names no price basis, and the nominal and real siblings are the unit token's options. Every token carries `segment` (`heading`, `byline`, or `departure`). A role with more than three entities reads as its count ("162 recipients"), and a role the request named as a group reads as that group ("the 27 EU member states"). The group rides on the Spec's `resolved.entity_groups`, and a client posts it as `entity_groups` beside the identity it resubmits. Otherwise the role binds the members, and the part's `group_read_as_members` departure names the residuals that total counts which the members leave out ("Africa, unallocated by country (DAC)"), and, for a group whose published total is not its members' sum, says the source publishes its own figure for the group. How the answer differs from the request lives on the part, in `departures`: each carries a closed `code`, the `binding_ref` of the token whose edit clears it, what was `asked`, what is `shown`, and a `text` of one or two short sentences ("You asked for 2030. The data ends in 2024, so this shows 1973 to 2024."). On a no-data part the text claims nothing is shown ("You asked for a ranking. Search does not compute rankings."). A named entity the series hold no values for is an `entity_unavailable` departure on its role's token ("You asked for Somaliland. The data holds no values for it."); a role named as a group counts its members without values and names none of them ("The data holds no values for 3 of the 27 European Union members."). A role that binds a named set's members beside another place carries no group, and still counts that set's members under the set ("You asked for Africa. The data holds no values for 9 of the 54 countries in Africa."), so the departure names only a place the request named by itself. A dropped phrase departs unless it is operation wording ("Trend in") or the part's heading and byline already state every word of it, and a dropped direction departs only when the request's own words gave it. A no-data answer whose check covered values the engine chose carries `scope_limited` ("This check covered only ODA."). `binding_ref` names the identity binding whose edit clears the departure; a client links it to the token with the same ref when the plan has one. Departures run in precedence order, from `closest_match` to `scope_limited`, and stay out of the spec sentence, which `ai` uses as the spec's label.

`resolved.unit_conversion` lists what a unit request can convert the answer to (`search.engine.unit.conversion`). Each entry of `currencies` carries the ISO code, the noun the byline names the currency by ("Guatemalan quetzales"), the run of served years a current-price and a constant-price request convert, and the first and last base years a constant-price request converts at. Those years and base years are the ones the conversion accepts (`ConversionAvailability`). A row reads the terms `materialise.convert.row_terms` lists: its native currency's rate in its year, and at current prices its target's rate in that year, or at constant prices its target's rate in the base year and the deflator of its entity on the recipe's deflator role in its year and the base year, the recipe's fallback entity standing in for an entity the table holds no row for. The native currencies are those of the facets the answer's read plan serves, so an answer bound to current US dollars served natively keeps the dollar's rates in later offers. The deflator entities are every entity the plan serves on the deflator role, bound or open, read from the plan and the coverage index in memory. A missing rate refuses the conversion, so the rates bound every row's years. A missing deflator leaves out only its entity's rows, so the constant-price years and the base years run wherever at least one served entity's deflator holds them, and a donor whose deflator starts late shortens no other donor's run. The period a unit request shows (`convertibility.unit_request_period`) reads the same availability. Beside the list sit the default base year and the national currency of each role bound to one entity. The offer is null for a stock, a series not in nominal prices, a unit that is no currency, a provenance with no recipe, or tables that convert no served year. Every fact comes from the conversion catalogue the app loads at startup and reloads when the graph generation changes (`search.engine.unit.conversion_catalogue`), so an answer makes no read for it. Startup waits at most 15 s for the first load and then serves while the load continues in the background. A failed load logs an ERROR and retries after 5, 15 and 60 s, then every 300 s. A reload requested during a load runs once that load ends, so loads run one at a time. While no catalogue is loaded, a currency answer carries the `unit_conversion_unavailable` warning, so a client can tell an outage from an answer that cannot convert.

A price-basis requirement the query names on `measurementQualifier` ("constant", "real", "at constant prices") is met by an answer served under a unit request whose price basis reads as one of the requirement's codes (`requirements._serves_price_basis`): constant prices meet `RealValue` and `InflationAdjusted`, current prices meet `Nominal`, and no unit request meets `PurchasingPowerParity`. So "GDP of Kenya at constant prices" served from a Nominal series converted to constant US dollars carries no `query_qualifier_dropped` warning or departure for "constant", and a candidate set meets the requirement only when every member is served so. The requirement still elects a publisher's constant-price series where one exists, and an answer served under no unit request, or at the other basis, leaves it unmet.

A unit request whose tables cover only part of the served years is answered over the longest run of years every needed table covers. The part carries a `date_coverage_partial` warning and a `year_unavailable` departure on `when:`: "Exchange rates start in 1980, so this shows 1980 to 2024." The offer's year runs state that period before the reader picks the currency. Inside that period a constant-price conversion leaves out each entity's rows in the years its deflator lacks, and every row of an entity whose deflator lacks the base year (`materialise.convert`). The mint's manifest and the answer carry a `deflator_missing_omitted` warning, and the part a `year_unavailable` departure on `unit:` naming the entities that lose the most years, at most three, then a count: "Constant prices leave out Taiwan before 2023, Azerbaijan before 2022, and Cyprus before 2018, which the price deflators do not cover." (`convertibility.deflator_omission_departure`). Names come from `ConversionCatalogue.entity_names`, which holds every entity a deflator table holds and every place with a national currency; an entity outside both, such as a multilateral donor, joins the count ("and 2 others", or "some years of 2 entities" when none is named). When the loaded tables cannot say whose rows the conversion leaves out (`counted_conversion` is `UNDECIDED`: no catalogue, served facets under several recipes, or no single deflator role), a constant-price answer still carries the warning and a departure naming no entity: "Constant prices may leave out years the price deflators do not cover." A row whose deflator-role cell is null names no entity, so its conversion refuses `rate_coverage_gap` with cause `deflator_missing`, as does a request left with no row.

Every slot menu on a definite answer is counted in memory before the Spec takes it (`search.engine.region.option_coverage.count_slot_options`). `SlotOption.served_rows` is the number of rows a pick's file would hold, in the same terms as `download.rows`: one per series and year inside the requested window, or under `latest` one per series holding its StatVar's latest year. Both read one code path, `CoverageIndex.series_years`, which ORs each series' year masks across the facets that serve it; the download sums the popcounts, and an option sums them over the series its pick reads. A sibling measure in `alternative_measures` carries the same count. `served_rows_exact` says whether the count equals the pick's own `download.rows`. It is false when a counted StatVar's series sit in more than one facet group (provenance, method and unit) in the window, since a per-series read elects one group per StatVar; when the answer reads in bulk and the pick's StatVars sit in more than one facet, since a bulk read elects one facet; under `latest`, when a StatVar has a facet observed more often than yearly, since the coverage index holds years and the read keeps the latest period; when the answer's identity binds a facet value (a unit, a provenance or a method), which the count does not apply, and for a probed option whose facets carry no complete date list. A unit request is applied: each counted series keeps the years its conversion keeps (`search.engine.unit.conversion.CountedConversion`), the years its entity's deflator holds at constant prices and none for an entity no currency names under a conversion into each entity's own currency, as `download.rows` counts them. The count is an upper bound under a unit request only when the loaded conversion tables cannot decide it (no catalogue, several recipes, a unit that is no currency, no single deflator or target role), when a counted series sits in a facet of a currency the answer does not serve, or when the coverage probe counts the menu. `download.rows_exact` and both `served_rows_exact` fields take their exactness from one rule (`counted_exact`). A same-shape merge ORs the masks of a series both answers serve. A StatVar the release's coverage view does not hold adds no rows, so the count covers the pick's other StatVars, and an option all of whose StatVars are missing from the artifact is omitted. A menu omits every option that would serve nothing except the slot's current value, so every option offered has data. The coverage probe under `include_option_coverage` runs only on menus the count leaves open: bound slots of graph-walked families, bound slots of subject-place families, and a menu whose count finds no series for any option while its members carry them. That last case means the coverage index and the members disagree, so it logs a WARNING and the probe counts the menu. The probe counts each option's plan as the download counts a standard plan (`download.pair_extent`).

The envelope itself carries a composed `rendered_sentence`/`sentence_plan` pair alongside `parts`, describing the whole query in one sentence. When every part is definite, the parts' headings are joined into one sentence. A qualifier (the counterpart, the subject place, the period, the source) that every part states once and alike, matched on text, connective, `binding_ref`, role, and `entity_refs`, is stated once after the joined phrases, in the family's own wording: "USA's health ODA and Britain's education ODA to Ethiopia." A possessive lead every part shares opens the sentence once ("Brazil's total population and nominal GDP, 1960 to 2024"), and parts that differ only in their lead share one noun ("USA's and Britain's health ODA to Ethiopia"). Entity names keep their case. A later phrase's first word is lowered only when it is plain Title Case. A qualifier that differs between parts stays in its own phrase, unless its connective opens with a comma (a period span), which would read as a list item inside the join. In that case, and when two parts' nouns are equal, each heading stays a sentence of its own, a heading two parts state alike is stated once, and the byline they share follows once: "Germany's gross ODA disbursements to Kenya, 2015 to 2020. Germany's gross ODA disbursements to Ghana, 2015 to 2020. Current US dollars · OECD Creditor Reporting System (CRS)." Beside other parts, a candidates part names its `variable_text` as an editable `what` slot before its own count of readings, an unsupported part follows that slot with its detail, and a no-data part whose reason quotes the variable frames the variable as that slot ("No match for left-handedness. Try a different wording or a related indicator."). Every other no-data sentence names what was checked itself and is mirrored. After the headings comes the byline, once when every part's byline reads the same, then each part's departures in part order, as `segment="departure"` tokens carrying that part's `part_index`. This is the one sentence that writes out a definite part's departures, since a spec's own sentence holds its heading and byline only. Every token in the composed `sentence_plan` carries a `part_index` pointing back into `parts`, or `null` for a token that belongs to the sentence as a whole (an inter-phrase connective, a shared qualifier, lead, or noun, the terminal period, the byline, the space between two per-part sentences, the coverage opener, an assumptions clause's lead, separators and full stop when no one part carries every item it names, or an assumption item more than one part carries). A single-variable query's composed sentence is that part's own sentence followed by its departures and its assumptions clause. Its `part_index` stamping nulls every qualifier and connective the lone part states, leaving its phrase under its own index.

Each part carries `assumptions`, every value the engine filled that the request did not ask for, as `{kind, binding_ref, text, refs}` in clause order, `refs` holding a declared default's value dcids (`search.render.assumptions`). A `declared_default` is a slot the request left open that its code system's or shape's declared default fixed, read from the trace's `declared_default` provenance of the part's own request, before a same-shape merge ranks its group's provenance, and named by the bound code's full label in the code index ("official development assistance", where the code's short name is "ODA"). An `open_role` is a flow role no request word asked for, named "every" and the role's noun in the answer's flow family ("every provider", "every creditor"). It is a role the role scope default filled (`resolved.scope` on that axis), whatever its member count, so a heading that reads "to Norway" because Norway alone holds data still states "every recipient", and a role the heading states with no entity of its own ("from 164 providers", "owed to all creditors"). A role the identity binds in `entities` or lists in `open_roles`, that one of the part's operations runs over ("top 5 donors" ranks over origin), or that the request's words asked every member of ("all donors", admitted by `region.result.every_member_phrases` and carried as `RegionResult.every_member_axes`), is asked and states none, as does a role a how value narrows ("owed to bondholders"). A `place_scope` is a request that named no place, read across every place with data, named by its members' types: "the world as a whole" for Earth alone, "every country" when every member is a Country, else "every place". A measure election (gross or net, disbursement or commitment), the price basis and an open year are no assumptions, since the heading's measure noun, the byline's unit and the period state them. A definite part reads its list off the Spec it serves, its declared defaults off its own request's Spec. A candidates part carries the items every list in its `reading_assumptions` holds alike, text included, and closes its own sentence with them. Its `reading_assumptions` holds each shown reading's own list under the reading's `spec_id`, the list a definite part answering that reading would carry, so an open role one reading reads, or an item two readings word differently ("every provider" against "every creditor"), is stated there alone. A no-data or unsupported part carries none. The roles and the place scope are read on every door. A `dataset_resubmit` identity entry posts every slot bound, so the engine records its slots as posted. The entry's `assumptions` echoes the part's list, or for a reading picked from a candidates part that part's `reading_assumptions[spec_id]`, and the rebuilt part states the echoed items that still hold, worded from the rebuilt Spec (`render.assumptions.assumptions_for`): a declared default whose slot still binds the echoed `refs`, an open role the answer still reads open, and the place scope of an answer that still names no place. An edit of a defaulted slot to another value drops that default, and an empty echo, from a part that stated none, states none. An entry with no `assumptions` states the open roles and place scope the rebuilt answer reads and no declared default, since the posted identity binds every slot. A `dataset_resubmit` candidates entry carries the same echo on each `ResubmitCandidate`, that reading's `reading_assumptions[spec_id]`, and the rebuilt part reads each reading with its own echo: its `reading_assumptions` entry states the echoed items that still hold, and the part's `assumptions` are the items every such list holds alike, so a year edit of a candidates answer keeps each reading's declared default and the clause keeps the shared one. A candidate with no `assumptions` reads as fresh. Parts that share a `spec_id` can differ in assumptions, so a client keeps each part.

The sentence that carries a part closes with its assumptions as one clause of `segment="assumption"` text tokens: "Assumed: official development assistance; every provider." A joined envelope sentence writes one clause after the departures, naming each part's items in part order, each item under its part's `part_index` and an item two parts share under none. A part others fold into also states the folded parts' own items, which each part builds from its own request. An item every one of them carries has no `part_index`, and an item one alone carries has that part's. A sentence written part by part closes each part's sentence with its own clause after its departures. A part whose sentence and clause both repeat an earlier part's is stated once with it, that sentence and clause under no `part_index`, followed by each part's own departures, and a part whose clause differs keeps its own sentence. A spec's own `resolved.sentence_plan` carries no clause. A mixed resolution with at least one definite part opens its envelope sentence with "Answered N of M parts.", N the definite parts, folded ones included, and M every part, as two `segment="coverage"` tokens with no `part_index`, the one segment no other token takes. A mixed resolution with no definite part has no opener.

On the SSE stream, every per-variable event (`shape`, `sentence`, `step`) carries `variable_index`, the same index the terminal `response` event's `parts` list is keyed by. Trailing per-spec events (`slot_options`, `entity_options`, `facet_options`, `observations`) join on `spec_id` instead, since a same-shape collapse can put two parts on the same spec. `sentence` events stay keyed by `variable_index`, one per part; the composed envelope sentence only ever rides the terminal `response` event, alongside the rest of the envelope. A part whose shape carries a measure family bind elected gets a first `sentence` event right after bind, naming only its measured thing from that election ("gross disbursements"), without the flow type and the year, followed by a second `sentence` event once materialise resolves the served rows, which adds both and refines that phrase when the served measure family differs from what bind elected. A part with no elected family (most indicators outside the CRS-style financial-flow axes) gets only the post-materialise `sentence` event, because materialise resolves its measured thing from the served StatVar.

#### Editing an identity

`kind: "dataset_resubmit"` with one `identity` entry posts a resolved identity back, edited or not. This is the shape a single-identity edit takes: `entries` carries one entry, and the envelope's one part is that identity re-resolved. `identity` is the complete definition. The engine derives the identity's StatVar members as the Datasets mint does, from the release's code index when it decides the identity, else from the mint's graph walk, which reads the whole filtered set up to `SEARCH_RESUBMIT_SUBJECT_CEILING` (20,000) subjects when its first page truncates without proof. A family holding a StatVar the index lists outside its code systems, a World Bank IDS series, is elected from the index's rows of its five-tuple in place of that walk, with no graph read (`code_index.members.listed_group`). A walk that runs that full read extends the request's deadline to `SEARCH_RESUBMIT_FULL_READ_TIMEOUT_S` (90 s) from its start. A family past the ceiling or the member cap answers an unsupported part, `scope_too_broad`. Reposting a Spec's `definition.identity` unchanged returns its `spec_id` again, because `spec_id` hashes only the identity core (`shape`, `slots`, `entities`, `open_roles`, `facets`, `requested_window`). Editing a bound entity role, here `identity.entities.observationAbout`, mints a new `spec_id` bound to the edited place:

```json
{
  "input": {
    "kind": "dataset_resubmit",
    "entries": [
      {
        "entry_kind": "identity",
        "variable_text": "population",
        "identity": {
          "identity_version": "spec1",
          "shape": {
            "kind": "statvar_schema",
            "population_type": "Person",
            "measured_property": "count",
            "stat_type": "measuredValue"
          },
          "slots": [],
          "entities": { "observationAbout": ["country/DEU"] },
          "requested_window": { "kind": "all" }
        }
      }
    ]
  }
}
```

The same document bound to France mints `spec_1da23159b03e3b3ca461fa2eaa552fb7`; this body, bound to Germany, mints `spec_ecb7cbe283c40ecbf80cbf045c934abd`.

An identity names a role it asks every member of in `open_roles`, a sorted list of `origin` and `destination` that is omitted when empty. A role is bound in `entities` or open, not both, and a pinned `stat_var` shape carries none. The answer's identity states the open roles its elected members serve. An open role in a posted identity is a promise of one series per member: where no elected member declares a role on that axis, the resubmit refuses `open_role_unavailable`, 422, at `/input/entries/{i}/identity/open_roles`. A fresh search that asks for the role elects whatever definition serves it, if one does. Each open role a candidate in a `candidates` entry carries refuses the same way, at `/input/entries/{i}/candidates/{j}/identity/open_roles`. On the parsed door an open role is a request: where it goes unserved, the identity omits it and the part warns `query_qualifier_dropped` with a `breakdown_not_applied` departure.

#### Editing a whole result with `dataset_resubmit`

`kind: "dataset_resubmit"` posts every part of a multi-variable result as one input and answers with the same N-part envelope, composed and folded across all N, so editing one part keeps the others' composed sentence and fold in sync. `entries` carries one entry per part, in response part order, each tagged by `entry_kind`. An `identity` entry carries the identity, plus a `variable_text` echo. A `candidates` entry posts a candidate set as a list of identities and gets them back re-materialised as a `candidates` part in the posted order, always, since that order records either an LLM's own ranking or a retrieval-score band, neither of which can be recomputed from the wire. A `placeholder` entry restates a still-unresolved sibling (`no_data`/`unsupported`) as a stub, scalars only. The engine never accepts a `NoData`/`Unsupported` payload back as input: posted state feeds verification, not the response. A `parsed` entry posts one `part` (a `ParsedPart`), ordinarily a prior response part's own `restatement` posted back edited, and is genuinely re-resolved through the parsed door's own per-part path. It is the entry for a sibling whose own text the user changed and which must be re-resolved rather than restated, unlike `placeholder`'s stub. There is no spec registry, so every entry carries its own full identity and the door reads no state from a prior request. `entries` is a full replacement, not a patch: the door is stateless and answers only with the parts it was posted. The response simply comes back with fewer parts when an entry for a part is omitted, rather than a refusal or a preserved copy of the missing part. A malformed entry fails the whole request 4xx, pointer `/input/entries/{i}/...`; a resolution outcome (`no_data`/`unsupported`) is a normal 200 part with siblings unaffected. When several entries are invalid, one rule decides which refusal a client gets. A static check (a placeholder entry's `code` against the code catalog, or a candidates entry's `candidates` length against the deployment's ceiling) always outranks a refusal only a live resolve can discover, and within either class the lowest entry index wins. One refusal therefore always names a single entry to fix, though fixing it can surface a further refusal from another entry on the next request.

#### Response cache and log line

The engine keeps complete answers in two layers. Layer 1 keys a `raw_text` question on its normalised text. Layer 2 keys it on the extraction output, and keys a `parsed` request on its whole input. Both keys add the request options, build commit, model, seed, UTC day, graph generation, coverage stamp, conversion catalogue revision, and the origin catalogue's `build_id` and `un_system_enabled`. A buffered request caches its response, with or without `include_observations` or `include_option_coverage`. An SSE request that sets either flag streams trailing events, and caches its events from `response` to `done` once `done` is sent, apart from its `place_options` events. An identical SSE request replays them with a fresh `response` and `done` and sends no `step`, `interpretation` or `sentence` event. Before `done` it streams place options as a fresh run does, under the same 1 s budget, from the options cache when it holds them (`POST /api/search/options`); they arrive after the replayed events. The two kinds of entry never serve each other, and an SSE request without either flag shares the buffered entry. A request that arrives while an identical one is computing waits for that one's answer, which for a streamed request means its `done`. An answer is not stored when it timed out, ended in an `error` event, ran a degraded fan-out leg, lost a read to a failure or deadline, or carries a probe-failure or dependency-unavailable warning (`response_cache_keys._UNSTORABLE_WARNING_CODES`). An `un_system_unavailable` warning blocks storage when it names a failed UN read in `params.variables`, carries no `params`, or names a place the UN front door refused while serving that the origin catalogue build's own refused set (`un_blocked_places`) does not hold; one naming only places in that set, a fact of the build in the key, is stored with the answer. A streamed answer is also not stored when it dropped a slot-options or observations event to a failure or deadline. Place options never block storage, since the entry holds none. `SEARCH_RESPONSE_CACHE_MAX_BYTES` (64 MiB) bounds the cache and `SEARCH_RESPONSE_CACHE_MAX_ENTRY_BYTES` (2 MiB) bounds one answer, and an answer held under both layers' keys counts once. A larger answer is served and not kept.

A `dataset_resubmit` request serves each `identity` entry from a per-part memo (`response_cache_memo.PartMemo`). Its key is the canonical JSON of the whole `ResubmitIdentityPayload` (the identity and its `entity_groups`), the four enrichment flags and the same stamps, so an edit to one part resolves that part and looks up the others. A hit rebuilds the part's trailing work from the current request, so a streamed hit still emits its `observations` event. A region with a lost read, an unsettled default scope or an unstorable warning is not stored. `SEARCH_RESPONSE_CACHE_MEMO_MAX_BYTES` (64 MiB, with 0 turning the memo off) bounds the memo and `SEARCH_RESPONSE_CACHE_MEMO_MAX_ENTRY_BYTES` (8 MiB) bounds one region. A region is weighed once, when stored, by the heap it holds (`response_cache_memo.region_bytes`), with the process-wide code, measure and coverage indexes counted as shared; a larger region is served and not kept.

The materialise bridge wraps its SDMX client in `CachingMaterialiseSdmxClient`, keyed on the read plan. `SEARCH_MATERIALISE_SDMX_CACHE_MAX_BYTES` (64 MiB) bounds the Arrow bytes it keeps and `SEARCH_MATERIALISE_SDMX_CACHE_MAX_ENTRY_BYTES` (8 MiB) bounds one read.

Every resolve ends with one INFO log line, `resolve ` followed by a JSON object: `kind`, `caller` (the User-Agent), each posted `include_*` flag, `transport` (`sse` or `buffered`), `cache` (`inactive`, `miss`, `hit layer1` or `hit layer2`), `memo_hits`, `memo_misses`, `total_ms`, `by_step` and `parts_by_step`.

### `POST /api/search/options`

Enumerates the candidate value set for one resolved entity role (`observationAbout`, `origin`, or `destination`) of a posted identity, on demand. It carries the same identity core a dataset_resubmit `identity` entry carries (`shape`, `slots`, `entities`, `open_roles`, `facets`, `requested_window`). The door derives the identity's own StatVar population server-side (`engine.options.population.options_population`), electing the role class under the identity's bound and open roles with the requested flow axis added as an open role (the identity an edit that binds that axis posts), so an IDS total asked for `origin` offers the creditors of its by-creditor twin. The population comes from the release's code index when it carries the identity's own five-tuple, or otherwise from the Datasets mint's member walk, which reads the whole filtered set up to `SEARCH_RESUBMIT_SUBJECT_CEILING` (20,000) subjects when its first page truncates without proof, or from the index's listed rows in its place for an IDS family, as the mint elects it. The option-set cache keys on that identity, so each requested axis has its own entry. It stores a result with its warnings unless a read failed during the compute (a coverage probe, or a UN read naming failures in its warning's `params.variables`), the UN front door refused a place the origin catalogue build's refused set does not hold, or the compute noted a degraded read; refusals of places in that set do not keep a result out. A streamed search's trailing `place_options` events read and fill the same cache under the same key, and the job that computes a scope fills it even when the stream has moved past its budget. It refuses `population_empty` when the identity's shape denotes no population, `scope_too_broad` past the walk's 700-member cap or its subject ceiling, `timeout` (504) for a walk past the request deadline, `invalid_role_ref` for a posted entity reference the graph does not carry, `option_target_invalid` for a role the resolved population's own declared role signature does not accept, `observationAbout` against a role-bearing family included, and `exact_binding_unsupported` for a posted slot bound `state="exact"`, which no Search door serves -- only the datasets doors read it. The refusal names the population's own accepted roles as alternatives rather than silently mapping the request onto the subject. `include_coverage` (default false) opts into a per-item observation-coverage probe; the default response carries plain candidate values only. Client interfaces use this endpoint to populate dropdowns and geographic peer selections. Each origin or destination item carries `party_kind` (`party`, `total` or `residual`), as do the items of `entity_options` and the origin and destination entities of an observations series. Search reads the code index (`CodeIndex.parties`, `materialise.enrichment.read_party_kinds`), and downloads add the graph type for a party the index does not list, so on Search such a party carries none.

### `GET /api/search/schema`

Returns JSON Schema definitions for `ResolveRequest` and `ResolveResponse`, including references to runtime capability descriptors.

### `GET /api/search/capabilities`

Returns runtime deployment capabilities, including supported recipe classes, active grammar axes, query character limits, and door liveness.

### `GET /api/search/catalogue`

Returns the published search catalogue: `{catalogue_version, counts, examples}`, the counts of indicators, datasets, publishers, countries and aid donors, recipients and years, and the example sentences the catalogue job checked against the engine, each `{text, template, stat_var, place}`. `?place=<country dcid>` returns the counts at that country and the examples whose StatVar has data there, filled with its name. A place that is not a `country/` dcid, or a country the catalogue does not count, refuses `request_invalid` (400). With no catalogue loaded the route answers `service_unavailable` (503) and every other route serves as usual. A 200 carries `Cache-Control: public, max-age=300`. See [deploy/search-catalogue.md](../../deploy/search-catalogue.md).

### `GET /api/search/healthz`

Returns service health status, the running build commit, and the capability version (`{status, build, capability_version}`).

## Testing and Evaluation

### Unit and Integration Tests

Execute the engine test suite with `pytest` or `just`:

```bash
uv run pytest tests/engine/
# or
just test tests/engine/
```

Tests cover:

- Direct pipeline execution and event stream validation
- Facet compatibility and refusal classification
- Near-neighbour candidate score disambiguation
- Options door resolution and surface generation

### Eval cases

Score the eval cases on a running app with the paired-gate driver:

```bash
just eval-app 8100
just eval-run http://127.0.0.1:8100
# or
uv run python scripts/eval_paired.py --single-url http://127.0.0.1:8100
```

The driver posts each eligible case to `/api/search/resolve` and prints every check that did not pass, per case. `just eval-paired` compares a head app against a base app. `scripts/README.md` lists the options.
